# ADR 030: Node Region Self-Attestation

**Date:** 2026-05-14
**Status:** Draft
**Touches:** [ADR 001](001-network.md), [ADR 008](008-reputation.md), [ADR 009](009-governance.md), [ADR 011](011-content-takedown.md), [ADR 012](012-client.md), [ADR 017](017-privacy.md), [ADR 019](019-node-onboarding.md), [ADR 026](026-gauge-boost-tokenomics.md), [ADR 028](028-slashing-appeals.md)

## Context

Each node declares a region — an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country code — at registration (`regionHint` on `StakingRegistry`) and in every gossiped `NodeAnnounce` (the `region` field on the signed body; see [ADR 001 § NodeAnnounce](001-network.md)). The declared region is load-bearing in four places:

- **Regional blacklist enforcement.** A node only applies blacklist entries that are global or match its declared region ([ADR 011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope)), and is only slashable for serving a hash blacklisted in its declared scope ([ADR 011 § Slashing — Regional slash eligibility](011-content-takedown.md#slashing)).
- **Gossip topic routing.** Nodes publish to the regional topic `cdn/region/{cc}/v1` in addition to the global topic ([ADR 001 § Gossip Topics](001-network.md)).
- **Peer-selection geo-diversity.** Clients prefer geographically diverse providers when other ranking factors tie ([ADR 001 § Selection](001-network.md), implemented in `crates/node/src/selection.rs`).
- **Appeals standing.** Operator standing under [ADR 011 § Blacklist Entry Appeals — Standing](011-content-takedown.md#standing) path 2 is gated on `node.region` matching the disputed entry's region.

Three ADRs ([001 § Consequences](001-network.md#consequences), [011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope), [019 § Deferred & Open](019-node-onboarding.md#deferred--open)) name "a decentralized oracle or third-party attestation service" as the production mitigation against region misreporting but provide no design — choice of oracle, IP→region resolution, mismatch handling, slashing implications, and operator UX are all unspecified. This gap is production-blocking and represents a missing design decision. Without a decision, regional compliance under [ADR 011](011-content-takedown.md) is structurally fragile and two region-flip gaming vectors — the appeals-standing flip ([ADR 011 § Standing](011-content-takedown.md#standing)) and the reactive blacklist-scope flip ([ADR 011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope)) — remain open.

This ADR closes the gap by **rejecting** the oracle/attestation-service path and committing to self-attested regions as the production posture, with one protocol-level hardening — a region-change stability window — that removes the two region-flip surfaces (appeals-standing and reactive blacklist-scope) that do not self-correct under the existing latency/reputation loop.

## Decision

### 1. Self-attestation is canonical

A node's region is whatever the operator declares — at `StakingRegistry.registerNode` time, in subsequent `StakingRegistry.updateRegion` calls (§ 3 below), and in every signed `NodeAnnounce` body. The protocol accepts the declaration at face value. There is no on-chain or in-gossip IP-geolocation cross-check, and no required signature from any third-party attestation service. Gossip-layer validation in `crates/gossip/src/validation.rs:127-130` remains shape-only (any two-byte ASCII-uppercase value). [ADR 001 § Gossip validation](001-network.md#gossip-validation) describes a stricter known-code-set check; tightening `validation.rs` to enforce it is orthogonal to this ADR and not a precondition — the divergence is logged in Cross-ADR Impact, not silently carried. No semantic geolocation gate is added either way.

### 2. Soft mitigation: latency-vs.-claim reputation penalty (canonical)

The reputation penalty described in [ADR 001 § Consequences](001-network.md#consequences) — clients apply a reputation penalty when observed latency contradicts the claimed region (default heuristic: RTT > 150ms to a node in the same claimed region) — is the canonical continuous mitigation. This ADR does not respecify the threshold, sample size, or decay curve; tightening those parameters is a follow-up in the [ADR 008](008-reputation.md) reputation domain, not a precondition for the decision recorded here — the residual control is consciously accepted in its current unparameterized form, tracked in Cross-ADR Impact. The penalty is self-correcting (a misdeclaring node loses payouts proportional to how badly its declared region contradicts measured RTT) and requires no new protocol surface.

### 3. Region-stability window

Two gaming surfaces are not addressed by the latency/reputation loop, because both pay off the instant a region is changed — faster than RTT sampling can tax the new claim:

- **Appeals-standing flipping** ([ADR 011 § Standing](011-content-takedown.md#standing) path 2): an operator flips `regionHint` immediately before filing a blacklist appeal to gain standing in any region. Today restrained only by multisig discretion ("heightened scrutiny on intake") — a norm, not a protocol invariant.
- **Reactive blacklist-scope flipping** ([ADR 011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope)): a node serving a hash flips `regionHint` out of the entry's region the moment a regional blacklist entry lands, shedding both the obligation to drop the content and — under [ADR 011 § Slashing](011-content-takedown.md#slashing) — the regional slash exposure for it. Today nothing gates this.

This ADR closes both with a single protocol invariant — a region-change stability window.

**StakingRegistry interface delta:**

- New field on the operator record: `regionLastChanged: uint256` (Unix timestamp in seconds, matching the existing `firstRegisteredAt`, `registeredAt`, and `lastMultiaddrUpdate` fields in [ADR 001 § Data Structure](001-network.md#data-structure)). Set to `block.timestamp` on every successful `updateRegion`; **not** written by `registerNode`, so it stays zero until an operator's first region change. Eligibility checks (§ below) read it with a read-time fallback for records with no region change yet — pre-upgrade records and freshly registered nodes alike: `effective = regionLastChanged != 0 ? regionLastChanged : max(firstRegisteredAt, regionGateActivatedAt)`, where `regionGateActivatedAt` is a single contract-global timestamp written once in the upgrade initializer that introduces this field. This avoids a per-record storage migration while staying conservative for the migration cohort: a pre-upgrade operator who changed region just before the upgrade does **not** inherit an ancient `firstRegisteredAt` reading that would pass the window instantly — the window runs from gate activation, not original registration (a bare `firstRegisteredAt` fallback would be *least* conservative for exactly the cohort most likely to be mid-flip at rollout).
- New field on the operator record: `regionPrev: string` — the region in effect immediately before the most recent `updateRegion`. Set to `""` on `registerNode`; set to the prior `regionHint` value on every successful `updateRegion`. Read only while a change is unripened (`block.timestamp - effective < REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW`); once the window elapses it is ignored. Shares the same `string` shape and the same deferred `bytes2` migration as `regionHint` (below).
- New operator-facing call: `updateRegion(string newRegion)`. Mirrors the operator-callable, no-bond shape of the existing `updateMultiaddrs` ([ADR 019 § NAT and Multiaddr Handling](019-node-onboarding.md#nat-and-multiaddr-handling); cooldown semantics in [ADR 001 § Multiaddr Update Policy](001-network.md#multiaddr-update-policy)) — caller must be the operator's registered Ethereum address. **`updateRegion` reverts if `regionLastChanged != 0` AND `block.timestamp - regionLastChanged < REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW`** (the cooldown equals the stability window). The `regionLastChanged != 0` guard exempts an operator's *first* region change from the cooldown, so a freshly registered operator is not locked out of correcting a mis-entered region for a window. This is safe: reactive evasion is foreclosed by the blacklist-scope predicate below (the previous region's entries keep applying via `regionPrev` for the window) regardless of the cooldown; the cooldown's sole job is preventing a *second* flip from outrunning the single `regionPrev` slot, and that second flip is still gated because `regionLastChanged != 0` once the first change has landed. A chain of rapid flips therefore cannot outrun the window, and at most one change is ever in-flight, so `regionPrev` is unambiguous. On success it sets `regionPrev = <prior regionHint>`, then `regionHint = newRegion` and `regionLastChanged = block.timestamp`. Emits `RegionUpdated(bytes32 indexed nodeId, string oldRegion, string newRegion)` — the `indexed` qualifier on `nodeId` matches every other `StakingRegistry` event in [ADR 001 § Interface (additions to StakingRegistry)](001-network.md#interface-additions-to-stakingregistry); the block timestamp is implicit in the event log header so a separate `timestamp` parameter would be redundant.
- Existing operator record field `regionHint` keeps its current `string` type and semantics; this ADR adds `regionLastChanged` alongside it without touching `regionHint`. [ADR 011 § Contract: ContentBlacklist](011-content-takedown.md#contract-contentblacklist) recommends `bytes2` over `string` for `region` as a production gas optimization; this ADR retains `string` here for consistency with the existing `StakingRegistry.NodeInfo.regionHint` shape ([ADR 001 § Data Structure](001-network.md#data-structure)), leaving the `bytes2` migration as a separate `StakingRegistry` storage-shape change orthogonal to the self-attestation posture.

Both amendments are keyed off the same `effective` timestamp:

```
effective = operator.regionLastChanged != 0 ? operator.regionLastChanged : max(operator.firstRegisteredAt, regionGateActivatedAt)
```

1. **Appeals-standing eligibility** under [ADR 011 § Standing](011-content-takedown.md#standing) path 2 additionally requires `block.timestamp - effective >= REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW`.
2. **Blacklist scope and slash eligibility** ([ADR 011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope), [§ Slashing](011-content-takedown.md#slashing)): a node is in scope for an entry iff the entry is global, OR `entry.region == regionHint`, OR (`block.timestamp - effective < REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW` AND `entry.region == regionPrev`). A region change adds the new region's entries immediately but does not drop the previous region's entries — or the slash exposure under them — until the change has ripened. Combined with the `updateRegion` cooldown above, a reactive flip after an entry lands buys nothing for a full `REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW`.

with `REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW = 7 days` (governable, hard bounds `[3d, 30d]` per the [ADR 009](009-governance.md) safety-bound pattern). For the *appeals* path, filings inside the window do not auto-revert — they remain admissible at multisig discretion only, preserving the existing soft-norm fallback for legitimate post-migration filers (datacenter relocation, ISP change) while making the protocol invariant strict by default. The *blacklist-scope* amendment is strict with no discretion path: the previous region's entries simply continue to apply until the change ripens. The contract enforces both strict paths; the multisig discretion path for appeals remains an off-chain governance norm and is unchanged by this ADR.

**Threshold rationale.** Seven days is short enough that an honest operator who genuinely relocates is not locked out of regional appeals standing for more than a week, and long enough that flipping region purely to gain standing costs seven days of latency-vs.-claim reputation in the *target* region while the timestamp ripens — comparable to the bond required to file. The 30-day upper bound matches the [ADR 028 § 5](028-slashing-appeals.md#5-hard-caps-and-frequency-limits) `APPEAL_FILING_WINDOW` default; the 3-day lower bound prevents governance from defanging the invariant (at 3 days the flip still forfeits three days of target-region reputation and three days of unservable old-region content, keeping it uneconomic relative to the bond). Interaction with [ADR 011 § Filing window](011-content-takedown.md#filing-window): `BLACKLIST_APPEAL_FILING_WINDOW` defaults to 14 days, hard bounds `[3d, 30d]`. The strict 7-day check sits inside the default filing window, but governance tightening the filing window below 7 days (permitted by its lower bound) creates a span where path-2 strict-rule standing is unavailable even though the filing window is still open; sub-window filings remain admissible at multisig discretion only, preserving the soft-norm fallback.

### 4. Misdeclaration is operator legal exposure, not a protocol offense

The protocol provides regional blacklist scoping as a *compliance affordance for honest operators*, not forced enforcement against dishonest ones. An operator who misreports region to evade a regional takedown remains the physical party subject to their actual jurisdiction's content law — courts reach the operator through staked TOKEN, registered Ethereum address, and registered multiaddrs without protocol-level geolocation. This is the posture every L1 takes toward miner/validator location: protocol provides mechanism; jurisdictions provide enforcement.

The residual exposure decomposes into two cases:

- **Reactive misdeclaration (flip after an entry lands).** Foreclosed by § 3: the previous region's entries — and the slash exposure under them — persist for a full `REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW`, and `updateRegion` is cooldown-gated, so flipping out of a region to dodge a fresh entry buys nothing. Appeals-standing flipping is foreclosed by the same window.
- **Pre-positioned misdeclaration (declare a false region from registration, before any entry exists).** *Not* prevented by the protocol, and § 3 does not claim to. A node that has always declared region `Y` while physically in `X` is, under [ADR 011 § Regional Scope](011-content-takedown.md#regional-scope) / [§ Slashing](011-content-takedown.md#slashing), simply out of scope for `X`-only entries — it sheds protocol slash exposure for region-scoped `X` takedowns. Misdeclaration *expands* slashing surface only when the disputed content is also globally blacklisted or blacklisted in the declared region; it does **not** for the canonical evasion case (an `X`-physical operator declaring a clean `Y` to dodge an `X`-only takedown), where the net effect is *less* protocol slash exposure, not more.

Backstops for the pre-positioned case are deliberately off-protocol: (a) declared-jurisdiction legal exposure (as above); and (b) the latency-vs.-claim reputation penalty (§ 2) taxes the false `Y` claim continuously. No new slashing offense for "region misreporting" itself is introduced — there is no protocol-level ground truth against which to slash, and inventing one would require importing exactly the oracle dependency this ADR rejects.

### 5. Privacy disposition unchanged

[ADR 017](017-privacy.md) classifies self-reported region (P-03, P-12, P-13) as accepted T2 exposure: intentionally public for client selection, inherent to the discoverability of a CDN serving regional clients. This ADR does not change that disposition. No new privacy surface is introduced — `regionLastChanged` is a timestamp, not new content, and is already implicit in the on-chain transaction history of `registerNode` / `updateRegion`; surfacing it as a structured field is a readability change, not a leakage change.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Closes the region-attestation gap with a concrete decision; supersedes the three "future work / production mitigation: oracle" passages in [ADR 001](001-network.md), [ADR 011](011-content-takedown.md), and [ADR 019](019-node-onboarding.md).
- No new external trust root and no new external dependency.
- Appeals-standing flipping *and* reactive blacklist-scope flipping become protocol invariants rather than, respectively, a multisig norm and an unmitigated gap.
- Operator UX unchanged for honest deployments — VPN, anycast, mobile, and multi-region operators are not penalized by an IP-geolocation gate that would systematically misclassify them.
- Compatible with the existing on-chain surface: `updateRegion` follows the operator-callable, no-bond shape of `updateMultiaddrs` ([ADR 019 § NAT and Multiaddr Handling](019-node-onboarding.md#nat-and-multiaddr-handling)), and `regionLastChanged` adopts the timestamp-field shape already used by `firstRegisteredAt` ([ADR 019 § Re-Onboarding after Deregistration or Auto-Ejection](019-node-onboarding.md#re-onboarding-after-deregistration-or-auto-ejection)) — with opposite mutability (updated on every successful `updateRegion`, vs. `firstRegisteredAt`'s write-once-on-first-registration).

### Negative

- The protocol cannot prevent a **pre-positioned** misdeclaring node (false region since registration, before any entry exists) from staying out of scope for the real region's entries. Reactive flipping is foreclosed by § 3, but a persistently false declaration is accepted at face value; this is the residual risk this ADR consciously accepts. Backstops are off-protocol — declared-jurisdiction legal exposure plus the continuous latency-vs.-claim reputation penalty (§ 2) — not protocol-level slashing: [ADR 011 § Slashing](011-content-takedown.md#slashing) routes slash eligibility under the *declared* region, which by construction does not reach a region the operator never declared.
- The multisig-discretion fallback for sub-7d appeal filings remains an off-chain norm. Operators with legitimate post-relocation filings still depend on multisig judgement during that window.
- The 7-day stability window slightly raises the cost of legitimate relocation when it overlaps a regional appeal — an operator who relocates and *then* wants to file in the new region must wait out the window or accept multisig-discretion intake.

### Risks

- **Coordinated region-spoofing attack on a region's reputation.** A fleet of misdeclaring nodes could pollute regional gossip topics and depress the region's measured reputation. Bounded by (a) the latency penalty (attacker reputation collapses as fast as RTTs are sampled), (b) the cost of staking each node ([ADR 026 § 7](026-gauge-boost-tokenomics.md#7-operator-economics-and-minimum-stake) minimum stake), and (c) gossip-bandwidth cost. Not free; self-correcting.
- **Governance-set `REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW` drift.** Lowering the window toward the 3-day floor weakens both the appeals-standing and the blacklist-scope protections. The hard bounds `[3d, 30d]` per [ADR 009](009-governance.md) safety-bound pattern make this an above-the-line governance question rather than a silent regression; the floor was raised from 1d to 3d specifically so the worst legal setting still keeps a region flip uneconomic (see § 3 Threshold rationale).
- **Migration-cohort window.** Pre-upgrade operator records have no `regionLastChanged`; the `max(firstRegisteredAt, regionGateActivatedAt)` fallback (§ 3) treats every pre-upgrade operator as having changed region at gate activation, so each must wait one `REGION_STABILITY_WINDOW` past activation before regaining path-2 appeals standing and before a region change ripens for blacklist scope. (A pre-upgrade record's *first* post-activation `updateRegion` is itself cooldown-exempt — `regionLastChanged == 0` — but neither ripens nor grants path-2 standing until the window passes, so the exemption changes timing of the call, not of the protection.) This is a one-time, bounded cost accepted in exchange for not running a per-record storage migration; it is the conservative direction (no record passes the window instantly on a stale timestamp).

## Cross-ADR Impact

- Tightening the [ADR 001 § Consequences](001-network.md#consequences) latency-vs-claim reputation penalty (threshold, sample size, window, decay) is in scope for the [ADR 008](008-reputation.md) reputation domain, not blocked by this ADR.
- Tracked spec/impl divergence: `crates/gossip/src/validation.rs` enforces only the shape check (two ASCII-uppercase bytes), not the [ADR 001 § Gossip validation](001-network.md#gossip-validation) known-ISO-3166-1-alpha-2 set check. Closing it is in scope for the [ADR 001](001-network.md) gossip-validation domain — logged here so the gap is not silent.
- Contract-level `regionLastChanged` / `regionPrev` / `regionGateActivatedAt` / `updateRegion` (incl. the stability-window cooldown) land with the `StakingRegistry` implementation; the § 3 interface spec is the canonical reference for that work.
